**This is where the instrument earns its keep.** With no corridor charge the authority cannot induce annual emissions below **740,311,297 kg**, however it builds — that number is a certified optimum, so it is a proved bound. With the state-contingent charge the same infrastructure reaches **691,362,556 kg**, which is 6.6% lower and within 1.0% of what a planner who could simply dictate operations would achieve. The priced figure comes from a solution the model exhibits, so it is attainable whether or not the search finished proving it minimal; a longer search can only move it down.

Every emission target between those two numbers is reachable with the charge and reachable with no amount of infrastructure without it. Pricing is therefore not a refinement of a corridor decarbonisation programme; it is a precondition for the tighter half of it.

India is the interesting case, and the interest is not geographical. The Central Electricity Authority publishes, in one edition of one database, an operating margin of 0.961 and a weighted average of 0.71. The operating margin sits above the whole threshold interval, so against it electrification raises CO2 for every admissible vehicle pair. The weighted average sits *inside* the interval, so against it the sign is not determined by the published data at all. Which factor a policy analysis picks decides the answer, and no number of additional case studies settles it.

The *social* ranking is a separate question. Electrification still lowers weighted social cost against India's operating margin at the central carbon price; the price at which that reverses is about 233 USD/t, which is inside the range the US EPA itself publishes. So this is a live policy question rather than a curiosity.

## 10. What not to conclude

- The instance is **calibrated, not measured**. Throughput, prices, emission factors and vehicle technology are published figures; the split of a corridor's volume across nodes and carriers is a documented, seeded rule. See `data/DATA_SOURCES.md`, which is explicit about which is which.
- Energy is accounted **per arc**. A battery-electric truck's state of charge along a path is not tracked, so the model gives an optimistic view of how little charging capacity suffices.
- The grid state is a **scenario, not an hour**. The charging-window question — when in the day to draw the energy — is one this model frames but does not answer.
- The emission threshold is a statement about a 40 t tractor-semitrailer drawing 1.36 kWh per km from the charger, not about electric trucks in general. The interval around it is wide because that figure is not well measured at 40 t, **not** because the grids are poorly measured. If one number in this package deserves a better source, it is this one.
- The outer grid states for every country except India are **constructed**, and their clean states are below anything those majority-fossil systems reach today. The *mean* of each country's tree is calibrated to its published annual average, so no aggregate result depends on the outer levels; but the state *spread*, which is what makes state-contingent pricing worth anything, is a construction for three of the eight countries.
- The regime matrix separates carbon from topology only in its **grid-only** panel. The full-regime panel also moves both fuel prices and the non-fuel haulage cost, which ranges threefold across these eight countries and is not a property of any power system.
- The corridor networks are comparable as modelling objects of similar structure, **not** as equally complete representations of their hinterlands. Rotterdam's and Manzanillo's absorb their ports' whole hinterland road volume onto a handful of nodes, so their arc volumes are high relative to reality.
- **Reach** is an optimistic measure: where the coalition is indifferent between optima the leader is assumed to get the one it prefers. The comparison between families is unaffected; the absolute levels are optimistic.
- The exact method is **not** needed for the recommended instrument. Theorem 2 makes the followers' objective identical to the social one, so that case is a single-level MILP. What needed exactness is everything about the *restricted* instruments.
- Where a result is an incumbent rather than a certified optimum, the `status` and `certified_mip_gap` columns of the result files say so. Attainability claims cite an exhibited solution and impossibility claims cite a proved bound; neither cites the other.
